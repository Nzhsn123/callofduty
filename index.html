Best ways to get your gameplay off console (or PC) and in here
PlayStation 5
  1. Press Share during/after the play to save the clip, or hold the PS button → Create → start a manual recording for something longer.
  2. From the Share menu, choose Upload → YouTube (needs your PS5 linked to a YouTube account once) — uploads straight from the console, no cables or USB needed.
  3. On a computer, go to studio.youtube.com → Content → find the video → click the ⋮ menu → Download. That's YouTube's own official feature for downloading your own uploads, so it gives you back the real file.
  4. Upload that file here.
Xbox
  1. Press Share for a quick clip, or Xbox button → Capture & share → Start recording for something longer (up to ~10 min internal, longer to USB).
  2. Open the Captures app → Manage → select clips → Copy to USB (format the USB as NTFS on a PC first).
  3. Easier alternative: on a Windows PC, open the Xbox app → your profile → Captures — downloads straight to the PC, no USB needed.
PC
  1. Already on your PC as a file — no transfer step needed.
  2. No extra software: Win + Alt + G (Xbox Game Bar, built into Windows) saves the last 30s to Videos → Captures as .mp4.
  3. NVIDIA/AMD GPU: the NVIDIA app (Instant Replay) or AMD Adrenalin (ReLive) can buffer much longer, up to ~20 min.
  4. For full control over a whole session, OBS Studio (free) records everything start to finish.

Upload requirements — read this first
Format
.mp4 (H.264) works best and loads everywhere. Avoid HEVC/H.265 .mov files if you can (the default on some iPhone recordings) — plays fine on Safari, but often fails to load at all on desktop Chrome/Firefox. Old-style .mpeg/.mpg files (MPEG-1/2) are a different format from MP4 despite the similar name, and browsers generally can't play them at all — convert to .mp4 first (e.g. with HandBrake, free).
Resolution
1080p is the sweet spot. Detection only reads a small cropped region of the frame, so 1440p/4K adds real processing time and memory risk without improving accuracy — if your device lets you choose a recording quality, pick 1080p.
Aspect ratio
Standard landscape (16:9). Portrait or ultrawide recordings aren't reliably supported — the detection zones assume a normal landscape gameplay HUD.
Length
No hard limit, but shorter uploads scan faster and use less memory. Trim to just the match(es) you want first if you can, especially on mobile.
On mobile
Works, but screen recordings can be hit-or-miss — high-resolution or very long recordings are the most likely to run into memory limits on a phone. 1080p and a trimmed-down clip are noticeably more reliable than a long 4K recording straight off the device.
While it runs
